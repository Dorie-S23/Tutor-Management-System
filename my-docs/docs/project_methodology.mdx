# Project Methodology

## Overview

The team follows an **Agile** approach, organizing work around a shared backlog and iterating in flexible-length cycles rather than fixed-duration sprints. Each cycle ends with a review/demo and a retrospective, and the next cycle's priorities are shaped by what came out of both.

Three meetings run every week: a standup, a sprint review/demo, and a retrospective.

## Weekly Meeting Cadence

### Standup

- **Purpose:** Quick sync on progress, plans, and blockers.
- **Format:** Each team member briefly covers:
  - What they completed since the last standup
  - What they're working on next
  - Any blockers they need help with
- **Length:** Kept short — this is a status sync, not a working session. Deeper discussion is taken offline or moved to a separate conversation.

### Sprint Review / Demo

- **Purpose:** Show completed work to the rest of the team (and stakeholders, if applicable) and confirm it meets expectations.
- **Format:**
  - Each contributor demos what they finished during the cycle.
  - The team discusses whether the work is genuinely done or needs follow-up.
  - Anything not finished carries over to the backlog for prioritization.
- **Outcome:** A shared, up-to-date picture of what's actually built versus what's still in progress.

### Retrospective

- **Purpose:** Reflect on how the team worked together, not just what was built.
- **Format:** Discuss, as a team:
  - What went well
  - What didn't go well
  - What to change or try next
- **Outcome:** Concrete action items the team commits to for the following cycle, not just a list of complaints.

## Cycles Instead of Fixed Sprints

The team doesn't work in fixed-length sprints (e.g. a rigid one- or two-week box). Instead, work is organized around the backlog and reviewed continuously:

- Tasks are pulled from the backlog as capacity allows, rather than being locked into a sprint at a fixed planning meeting.
- The weekly review/demo and retro still provide the same rhythm a fixed sprint would (regular check-ins, regular reflection), without forcing every piece of work into an artificial weekly boundary.
- Larger features may span multiple weeks; smaller fixes may be picked up and finished within days.

## Branching, Commits & Pull Requests

Day-to-day version control follows the team's separate [GitHub Collaboration Methodology](./Github_Methodology.md) — feature branches off `develop`, conventional commit messages, and peer-reviewed pull requests before anything merges.

## Definition of Done

Before something is marked complete and demoed in the sprint review, it should:

- Build and run without errors
- Pass all relevant tests
- Work as intended when demoed
- Have been through code review and merged (per the GitHub methodology)

## Communication Between Meetings

- Blockers raised in standup that can't be resolved on the spot are followed up on outside the meeting, not left until the next standup.
- Major scope or architectural decisions are discussed with the team before being acted on, rather than being saved for the next scheduled meeting.
